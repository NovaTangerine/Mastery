## 🟢 Stakeholder Summary: Product Positioning & Viral Loop

### Core Philosophy: The Anti-Backlog
Cartridge moves away from the "guilt-tripping" nature of traditional backlog trackers. Instead of a list of chores, it functions as a digital trophy room and a celebration of active engagement and completion.

### The Viral Hook: "The Digital Postcard"
* **Visual Identity:** Shared content is designed to look like a postcard or scorecard.
* **User Psychology:** It evokes the feeling of adding a trophy to a wall—a compelling, high-fidelity window into a specific playthrough.
* **Utility:** It serves as a "micro-narrative" that encourages non-users to see the depth of a friend's gaming experience without needing to read a long review.

### Exit States & Cartridge Eligibility
To maintain a constructive community culture, the system generally restricts Cartridge generation to positive milestones. This prevents the "viral engine" from becoming a primary vehicle for negativity.

| Exit State | Description | Cartridge Eligibility |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Completed** | The user finished the game. | Standard. The primary way to earn a "Trophy." |
| **Backburnered** | Enthusiasm waned, but the user may return. | Locked. Encourages the user to return to "Complete" status. |
| **Abandoned** | The user definitively stopped playing. | Gated / Restricted. (See below). |

### The "Extraordinary Disappointment" Exception
While the platform avoids "hate-tracking," it recognizes that dropping a game can sometimes have intellectual value (e.g., a sense of betrayal or deep mechanical failure).
* **Scarcity Logic:** Users are generally discouraged from generating Cartridges for abandoned games.
* **Limited Allocations:** A limited number of Cartridge generations may be granted for "Abandoned" states.
* **Intent:** This ensures that when a user does choose to commemorate a failure, it carries weight and provides unique insight rather than just being a low-effort complaint.
