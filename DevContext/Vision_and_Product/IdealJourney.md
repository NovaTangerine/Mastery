# Ideal User Journey:

### 1. Set Currently Playing
    
  After the user signs up, they’ll be taken to a “newbie” landing page composed of unique, compelling empty states for content modules that will be filled with user-focused activity once they’re a more mature/active user. The modules are designed to get users to engage with key parts of the platform and get themselves invested in the community early on. These modules include: What Are You Currently Playing, Set Your Top Games, Complete a Basic Profile, Write Your First Review and/or Generate Your First Cartridge
    
  Sets the user up for Logging, Reviewing, Posting, and Answering Topics on the game they select.
    
### 2. Customize Profile
    
  While the full user profile will offer robust and varied options for users to express themselves, the onboarding experience will prioritize users adding an avatar and writing a short bio to make themselves presentable on the platform.
    
 Sets the user up for sharing their content on the platform. Things like avatars and bios will probably be included in various ways depending on the type of content the user posts. Having those elements filled out will make them identify with the community more, and will make them more recognizable and authentic to the other users.
    
### 3. Set Top Games List
    
  User can select their Top 3/5/10 Games, write tags on the games, and share the list of games to their profile. This step combined with the profile customization step will give users the baseline to look/feel like functioning members of the community.
    
  Sets the user up for early data collection (start curating user landing page + social recs based on currently playing + top games list selections), building their presence/brand, and giving them a pool of games to easily pick from future reviews/logs/posts.
    
### 4. Answer Topics
    
  With their top games and currently playing set, users can now easily start answering Topics for the games on the list. Prompt the user to answer some easy low-hanging fruit questions about the game so they’ll have content to post about or generate alongside cartridges later on. Topics should be thoughtful (we want to draw out unique user POVs), but easy to answer. Think of questions like “how does this game compare to others in the series/genre?”, “describe the most impactful moments of this game” *-eh, I need better examples. will come back to this later*
    
  Topics are more of a mature user behavior (as in, these probably won’t ever be the first or the primary feature that users come to the site for, but it can offer a lot of value for users who are already invested in the platform. Answers to topics should be analyzed and tagged by AI and used to draw users together who will stimulate and challenge each other and create interesting, thought-provoking content that draws others users in. Key answers to topics should be integrated into a user’s profile somehow as a proof of their activity on the site and as a stamp of authenticity for how they engage with games. Compared to Posts, Topics are game-specific and a little more in-the-weeds. A user might choose to elevate a Topic they answered by turning it into a post on their profile. A user might also integrate multiple Topic answers into a review or a generated cartridge.
    
### 5. Post About / Tag Games
    
  Think of Posts as content found more on traditional social media platform. Broad, short-form, non-specific content that’s representative of a user’s brand and personality rather than representing any singular topic. Currently conceiving of posts as something like a tweet or a tumblr post. Posts are probably one of the easiest/lowest-effort interactions a user can have with the platform in the sense that the user doesn’t have to have actually even played a game to post a thought about it. Posts can also create a sort of connective tissue between the various features on the product. While a user might review games, journal games, and generate cartridges, their feed of posts might be the primary place where they combine outputs from all of these different functions into a digital presence that feels fascinating and magnetic.
    
  Posts also exist to create a sort of synergy between the product and other social media networks. Currently, internet users are often funneled by default into posting about gaming on traditional social media channels. This product must be aware of that and offer an experience that feels familiar and doesn’t force the user to abandon these traditional channels (built-in audiences from other platforms will always be a huge draw in gaming because the time investment required to complete single-player games doesn’t always lend itself to immediate, organic relationship-building and social interaction). Users should easily be able to turn posts into tweets without having to do a lot of adjusting.
    
  After creating posts and tagging a few games (and maybe answering some Topics), users should have a decent base of game data to make creating/sharing a few cartridges easy.
    
### 6. Generate Cartridges
    
  Cartridges are a flagship feature of the product. The premise is basically that the user can generate these interactive cards as a sort of summary/commemoration of of their playthrough of a game. At minimum, the user just needs to enter the game played, difficulty played, hours played, platform played on, a rating for the game, and maybe a few tags, and the product will generate this into a card that feels fun and interactive. It’s a fun way of sharing and visualizing your unique experience with your game library at its core.
    
  However, if a user is truly invested in the product, they can integrate other items like Posts, Reviews, Tags, and Topics into their cartridge to make it an even more robust, interactive, and unique artifact. While Cartridges themselves are a unique selling point of the platform, they offer an even more thrilling and unique experience as a reward to power users who engage deeply with games on the platform.
    
### 7. Review Games
    
  While reviews seem somewhat in the background of the product due to all of the other unique functionality and experiences that we build to draw in users, reviews are actually a core interest for me and for the product. Ultimately, I’d love if users came in and just decided to start reviewing games out of their own self-driven motivation. And that is a totally valid and supported use-case. However, for users who aren’t ready to take the step of writing their own reviews, all of the other product functionality provides an easy scaffold for users to build a review based on their own game journals, topics, and tags.
    
  Any user can come in and start rating games, tagging games, answering questions, and generating cartridges. However, my hope in creating all of that stuff is that they will help users get in touch with their inner thoughts to the point they feel confident enough to write a review. Writing a review is probably the purest expression of true engagement with the platform, because it shows a willingness to create something informational, expressive, or entertaining for others without necessarily expecting anything back.
    
### 8. Journal Games
    
  Having gone through the effort of creating their profile and adding, rating, and generating cartridges for some of their top games, the user realizes how unique the platform is and is invested in logging their next game on the platform. They open the product alongside starting their new playthrough of a game so that they can jot down all manner of observations in the journal while they play. They turn those journal thoughts into posts and tags for the game, and their experiences while playing inspire them to answer a few Topics, too. Once they finish the game, they have a ton of a data for the product to pull from, allowing them to make a really in-depth cartridge with lots of interactive sections explaining their thoughts and pulling in their posts and Topic answers.
    
  Sometime after generating the cartridge for the game, they decide to revisit it and post a full review of their thoughts. When they go to review the game, there’s already so much information from their posts, tags, journals, and topic answers, that the system is able to recommend several sections and themes for their review. The user spends 10-60 minutes writing the review and making it feel like their own. Then they post the review to their profile!
